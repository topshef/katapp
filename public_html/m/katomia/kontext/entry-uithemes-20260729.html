1 · What the UI has to say

Katomia's pitch line is "swap things, not prices" — multi-party value exchange as a social, collaborative game, not a terminal. The audience you named: people open to exploring the new, comfortable with fuzzy boundaries, in it partly for the fun. So the UI's job is to signal, within seconds:

  • This is alive — things move organically, respond to you, feel liquid (the core metaphor: rigid assets → pooled liquidity).
  • This is a game, but with real stakes — playful surfaces, serious moments (signing, mainnet) rendered with calm precision.
  • This is new territory — it shouldn't look like a DEX clone or a SaaS dashboard; one unmistakable signature beats a themed everything.

Design principle that follows: own a gesture, not a palette. Sites are remembered for their motion identity (how it feels to touch) far more than their colour scheme — which is also far cheaper to retrofit onto the existing app than a full re-theme.

4 · The signature gesture — living borders (built, try it)

Your hover-ripple idea is real now: the border is a closed elastic loop (a wave equation runs around the perimeter). Hover blows at the bubble from the pointer's position; click is a harder puff; idle keeps a barely-there float so it feels alive. Three stroke voices — ink (clean), glow (blurry), iridescent (soap film) — settle the clean-vs-blurry question empirically: the demo page has all three plus physics sliders (tension/spring/damping/puff/ambient). It degrades to the plain CSS border under prefers-reduced-motion or no canvas — garnish by construction, never load-bearing.

Where it could live beyond buttons (each is the same loop physics on a different surface): the ring board's outer edge breathing; a droplet-merge when an offer lands in a basket (two loops coalescing — the intro-animation language at micro scale); a "surface tension" snap when a cluster locks; meniscus-style progress on the strike-delay countdown. One family of motion = a brand.

5 · Recommendation

Liquid worn lightly. Keep Paper as the working base for the alpha (dense info, mobile legibility, zero risk), and make the liquidity language the brand layer on top:

  1. Adopt kbubble on hero elements only — welcome cards + primary CTAs (iridescent for the hero, glow for CTAs). One view, one soap film.
  2. Liquid glass becomes the "world" skin — intro animation (ENTRY-INTRO1), marketing pages, maybe a night-mode board later. It's the pitch deck made navigable, not the default app chrome.
  3. Steal Linear's calm for the money moments — signing modal and mainnet confirmations stay austere; the contrast is the trust story.
  4. Let testers vote — the nerd-mode picker is already a feedback instrument; wire the choice into feedback context later so preferences arrive labelled.

Clay is the fallback if alpha feedback says "too cold"; teenage-style brutalism is the pole to revisit if it says "too soft".
