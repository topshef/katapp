1 · The message, one line

Rigid, disconnected assets become one liquid, organic pool. Squares (the blank, brittle web; order-book grids; siloed tokens) melt, flow together, and coalesce into a living ring of liquidity — and you enter Katomia through the ring.

3 · Video-generation prompts

Written for current text-to-video models (Veo / Runway Gen-4 / Sora-class; also fine as a Blender brief). Generate 5s and trim; ask for a locked-off camera until the final push-in.

Main prompt

A minimalist abstract product intro, 5 seconds, 4K, photoreal CGI.
Open on a pure white frame. A precise grid of small flat matte squares
fades in, barely visible, like ceramic tiles on a white wall. The squares
slowly gain depth, extruding into small glass cubes at randomly staggered
distances from camera, while the background dims from white through grey
to near-black. The cubes take on a cold, icy translucent blue.
In zero gravity the cubes MELT in place: edges soften and each cube sags
into a wobbling water droplet. Drifting droplets that touch merge like
mercury (metaball behaviour). Then every droplet is drawn toward the
centre of frame by an invisible force and streams onto the surface of one
large floating TORUS — the proportions of a car tyre with a wide open
hole — whose surface is a thin iridescent soap-bubble film: oily rainbow
interference colours shimmering over deep black. The torus floats like a
bubble from a child's bubble wand: gently oscillating in every dimension,
tube thickness pulsing unevenly, the ring flexing slightly elliptical,
slowly precessing in space — organic, never mechanical.
Camera: locked off until the final second, then a slow smooth dolly
forward through the centre hole of the torus into black.
Lighting: dark studio void, soft rim light, no floor, no text, no logo.
Mood: calm, premium, liquid, hypnotic. No cuts.

Useful variants

  • Logo beat (if the model handles it — else composite in post): …as the camera approaches, a minimal white letter "K" glows faintly in the black centre of the ring.
  • Condensation flavour (your window-droplets image): …the melting reads like condensation on cold glass: droplets tremble, run, and swallow their neighbours, leaving brief wet trails.
  • Evaporate–condense alternative (the other path you described): cubes blur into Gaussian mist, the mist condenses back into droplets, then coalesces — dreamier but risks reading as "smoke"; the straight melt is the safer pun.
  • Loop version for the welcome background: skip the square prologue; just the floating oscillating torus, seamless 8s loop, very low contrast.

Consistency tip: generate the final torus as a still first (match the pitch cover), then use it as an image-conditioning keyframe for the video pass so deck and site agree.

4 · Doing it live: WebGL feasibility

Verdict: very feasible at illustration quality; the physics story maps 1:1 onto standard techniques.

BeatTechniqueCost
Tile grid → cubesInstanced quads/boxes, per-instance Z offset + colour ramp uniform driven by a master timelinetrivial
Melt + merge2D route (recommended first): metaballs via a blurred additive canvas + threshold shader — classic, cheap, mobile-safe. 3D route: ~2–5k particle SDF, raymarched with smooth-min union (the standard "liquid blob" shader) — fine on desktop GPUs, needs a quality knob for phones.the crux; both routes battle-tested
Coalesce to ringParticles seek nearest point on a torus SDF (length(vec2(length(p.xz)-R, p.y))-r) — i.e. the attractor is the torus, exactly your "gravity is the ring" idea. Spring + drag + curl-noise jitter.cheap
Organic torusDisplace R and r with low-frequency sinusoids/noise in the SDF (tube pulse, ellipse wobble, Z-precession). Iridescence: cheap thin-film approximation from fresnel → spectral ramp.cheap
Fly-throughCamera dolly along +Z through the hole; crossfade to the page.trivial

Suggested build path: (1) the 2D sketch below (shipped in this doc) to sign off timing/beats → (2) three.js + raymarched-SDF prototype behind /welcome?intro=1 → (3) if a video is still wanted, render the same scene offline at high sample counts — one asset pipeline, two outputs, which is also your "generate the video by rendering it finer" instinct. Libraries worth stealing from: three.js MarchingCubes example, "webgl-fluid" style thin-film shaders. Respect prefers-reduced-motion and cap at ~3s + tap-to-skip.
